What We Do, Specifically

Most "calculator" sites optimize for ad impressions. They slow pages down, hide features behind pop-ups, and make the result secondary to the surrounding chrome. HandyTool is built the other way around: the calculator is the page, and everything else is in service of using it. If a tool takes more than a few seconds to answer a real question — what will my mortgage payment be, how much tax will I owe on this receipt, is my 401(k) enough — we treat that as a bug to fix.

Our tool lineup today:

  • Tip Calculator — restaurants, delivery, taxis, group splitting, currency flexibility
  • 401(k) Calculator — retirement projection with employer match, catch-up contributions, and real-return modeling
  • Mortgage Calculator — P&I plus taxes, insurance, PMI, HOA, with an amortization schedule
  • Loan Calculator — personal, auto, and student loans with extra-payment scenarios
  • Sales Tax Calculator — all 50 US states with current base rates

Our Methodology

Every calculator uses the standard formulas published by the originating authority — the Internal Revenue Service, the Federal Reserve, state departments of revenue, or the lender industry's amortization standard. We cross-check against publicly available calculators from trusted institutions and publish the formulas we use so they can be audited.

Our Principles

Fast by Default

Every page ships as a single HTML file with inline CSS, no framework, and no blocking third-party scripts beyond analytics and the ad library. Typical first-contentful-paint on 4G mobile is under 1.5 seconds; on broadband, under 400 ms. Calculators run entirely on the client, so results appear instantly without a round-trip to any server.

Inputs Stay in Your Browser

Bill amounts, income figures, loan balances, and other inputs you enter are never transmitted to our servers. All math runs in JavaScript on your device. Preferences you set — default currency, rounding mode, tip percentage — are saved to your browser's local storage so they persist across visits, but they stay on your device. For details see our Privacy Policy.

Mobile First, Not Mobile-Last

Over half of our visitors use a phone. Every tool is designed phone-first with 44-pixel-minimum touch targets, high-contrast type, and a layout that works equally well at a restaurant table, on a train platform, or in a lender's waiting area. Large-screen users get more horizontal room for side-by-side scenarios, not a different experience.

Accurate, Honest, and Correctable

Every calculator is tested against known reference results before shipping. We correct mistakes publicly in the page footer's "Last reviewed" line. If you spot a wrong formula, a bad rate, or a UX glitch, email us and we'll fix it, usually within a day. Our Contact page is the fastest path.

Editorial Standards for Our Guides

Alongside the calculators, we publish in-depth guides on topics like 15-year vs 30-year mortgages, Roth vs Traditional 401(k), and the real cost of long-term auto loans. These are written to help readers make concrete decisions, not to rank for broad keywords.

Each guide follows the same rules:

  • Specific numbers, not vague ranges. Where we quote rates, limits, or percentages, they're drawn from the current source document and dated.
  • The trade-off is always named. A guide that says "do X" without explaining when X is wrong is marketing, not advice.
  • Real scenarios. Each major guide includes 3–4 worked examples across income levels and life stages, not a single generic case.
  • External sources linked inline. When we cite an IRS rule, a Federal Reserve rate, a CFPB consumer guidance, or a state agency document, the source is linked directly.
  • Reviewed and dated. Each guide shows when it was first published and most recently reviewed. The entire catalog is re-reviewed on a rolling basis.

Who We Are

HandyTool was founded in May 2026 by a small team with backgrounds in software engineering and financial-services product work. We are not a venture-backed startup, a legacy media brand, or a content-arbitrage operation. The site runs on a modest budget covered by advertising and a small set of affiliate relationships; the team is deliberately small to keep editorial decisions in the hands of the people who use the tools.

We're based in the US and write primarily for US-based readers, which shapes every calculator (tax jurisdictions, retirement account rules, tipping norms, mortgage conventions). We'll add international support where it's technically straightforward and useful — the Tip Calculator supports multiple currencies — but we won't pretend to be a global authority on tax codes we haven't audited.

How HandyTool Makes Money

Full transparency about our revenue model matters, both as a trust signal and because it affects which tools we build and which we don't.

  1. Display advertising via Google AdSense. A small number of ad units appear on each page, placed where they don't interfere with using the calculator. Ads fund hosting, tool development, and editorial maintenance.
  2. Affiliate partnerships, limited. On some guides, we link to financial products we've reviewed and would recommend. If a reader opens an account through such a link, we may earn a referral fee. This is disclosed inline on each page where it applies. We don't change recommendations based on commission size; the first filter is always "is this actually the right product for most readers of this guide?"

What we don't do: we don't sell personal information, run sponsored content, write paid reviews, or allow advertisers editorial input. We don't take payment to add or remove products from a calculator's defaults.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the calculators accurate?

Yes, within the scope stated on each page. Our mortgage, loan, and 401(k) calculators use the standard formulas published by the originating authorities and have been cross-checked against the reference calculators listed in the methodology table above. State sales-tax rates use the base rate only — if your city or county adds a local surcharge, the actual tax at the register will be slightly higher. Our tool notes this where relevant.

Do you save my inputs?

No. All math runs in your browser and the inputs you type are never sent to us. The one exception is preference-type settings (default currency, tip percentage, rounding mode) that are stored locally in your browser so they persist across visits. Those stay on your device and we don't see them.

Why is the site free?

The operating cost is modest — a handful of static HTML files on Cloudflare — and advertising plus occasional affiliate referrals cover it comfortably. Charging users would require sign-ups, account management, and support infrastructure that would run against the "open a page, get your answer" design.

Is this financial advice?

No. Our calculators and guides are educational tools. Personal financial decisions — especially ones involving retirement accounts, mortgages, or taxes — involve specifics about your situation that only a licensed professional can fully account for. Treat our output as a starting point for a conversation with a fiduciary advisor, CPA, or mortgage professional, not as the final word.

How do I report a bug or suggest a tool?

Email contact@gethandytool.com. Include the page URL, what you typed, what you expected, and what actually happened. We read every message and most fixes ship within one or two days. New tool suggestions go on our roadmap; we build out the ones the most readers ask for.

Do you have an API?

Not publicly, no. Our calculators run in the browser and don't involve server-side math, so there's no API to expose. If you're building a tool that needs similar math, the formulas on our Methodology section are a good starting point — they're all standard published formulas, not proprietary.
